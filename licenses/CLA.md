Thank you for contributing to Cilica. This agreement sets out the rights you give the maintainer in your contributions. You keep the copyright in your work. You give the maintainer a broad license to use it, so Cilica can stay open source under the AGPL and also be offered under paid licenses that pay for its development.

You accept this agreement by signing it through the CLA Assistant bot on your first pull request. If you contribute on behalf of a company, see section 7.

## 1. Definitions

- **Maintainer** means Neo Haggard, and any company Neo Haggard sets up to run Cilica, or anyone to whom the Cilica project is later transferred under section 9.
- **You** means the individual signing this agreement, or the company named in a signature under section 7.
- **Project** means the software, documentation and other material the maintainer publishes as Cilica, including its desktop app and its server.
- **Contribution** means any original work of authorship, including changes to existing work, that you submit to the maintainer for inclusion in the project, by pull request, patch, issue, comment or any other means. Anything you clearly mark in writing as "Not a contribution" when you submit it is excluded.
- **Submit** means send to the maintainer or the project's repositories, issue trackers or other channels.

In return, the maintainer agrees that any version of the project that includes your contribution will also be made available under the license the project used for that part on the day you submitted it (the GNU AGPL v3 for the desktop app, or the Cilica Server License for the server), or under another license approved by the Open Source Initiative.

As far as the law allows, you waive, or agree not to assert, any moral rights in your contributions against the maintainer and anyone who receives the project from the maintainer.

## 6. What you promise

You promise that:

1. each contribution is your original work, or you have the right to submit it under this agreement;
2. if your employer or anyone else has rights in work you create, such as under an employment contract or policy, they have allowed you to make the contribution under this agreement, or have signed it under section 7;
3. you will mark clearly any part of a contribution that is not your own work, and say where it came from and under what license (for example, "Includes code from Example Library, MIT License"); and
4. you will tell the maintainer if you learn that any of these promises is no longer true.

## 7. Contributing for a company

If you contribute as part of your job, your employer may own the copyright in your work. In that case your employer should sign this agreement as the contributor. An authorised person should contact the maintainer through <https://cilica.app>, name the company, and confirm in writing that they are authorised to sign for it. The company then makes the grants and promises in this agreement for contributions submitted by its employees, and should tell the maintainer which GitHub accounts contribute on its behalf.

You do not have to provide support for your contributions. ***Except for the promises in section 6, you provide your contributions as is, without warranties or conditions of any kind, including title, non-infringement, merchantability or fitness for a particular purpose.***

## 9. Transfer

The maintainer may transfer this agreement, and the rights you grant in it, to anyone who takes over the Cilica project. Whoever takes over must keep the promise in section 3.

## 10. General

This agreement covers all your past and future contributions to the project. If a court finds part of it unenforceable, the rest still applies.
